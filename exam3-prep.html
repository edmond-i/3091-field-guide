Last-hour facts: tick only what you can say without looking

The twelve ways people lose points on this exam

  1. Using the coefficients as rate-law exponents for a reaction that is not a single step.
  2. Forgetting that only the second-order plot (1/[A] vs t) has slope +k; or steeper Arrhenius line = “faster” (it means larger Ea).
  3. Using kJ with R in J, or ΔS in J/K with ΔH in kJ; Celsius instead of kelvin.
  4. Thinking ΔG < 0 means fast, or that a catalyst changes K, ΔG or the equilibrium yield.
  5. Getting the sign of W wrong (work done on the system is +).
  6. Writing K as reactants over products, including solids, or comparing Q and K the wrong way round.
  7. Forgetting that an exothermic reaction has a smaller K at higher T (van’t Hoff).
  8. pH arithmetic: sign of the log, pH + pOH = 14 only at 25 °C, lower pKa = stronger acid.
  9. Henderson–Hasselbalch upside down (it is [A−]/[HA], base over acid); assuming every equivalence point is pH 7.
  10. Mixing molarity (L of solution) with molality (kg of solvent).
  11. In Ksp problems: reporting x instead of [ion] (Ca3(PO4)2: 3x and 2x), forgetting to halve concentrations on mixing, or comparing Ksp across different formula types.
  12. Not giving a reason in “briefly explain” parts, and eliminating units.
