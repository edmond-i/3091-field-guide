Last-hour facts: tick only what you can say without looking

The ten ways people lose points on this exam

  1. Answering with the electron geometry when asked for the shape (XeF4 is square planar, not octahedral).
  2. Calling a molecule nonpolar because it has polar bonds, or polar because it has a lone pair (XeF4, XeF2 are nonpolar).
  3. Forgetting the ½ in bond order, or filling N2 with the O2 orbital order.
  4. Using the wrong touching line for r ↔ a (edge, body diagonal, face diagonal).
  5. Using 2θ in Bragg’s law, or a calculator in radian mode.
  6. Using d = a/√(h²+k²+l²) on a non-cubic crystal, or confusing r, a and d.
  7. Forgetting that a peak must satisfy the selection rule (FCC: all odd or all even).
  8. Not giving a reason in “briefly explain” parts (strained Si: longer bond, weaker, smaller gap).
  9. Reversing n and p (compare the dopant’s valence electrons with the atom it replaces).
  10. Eliminating units: eV with J, Q in kJ with R in J, cm³ vs m³.
